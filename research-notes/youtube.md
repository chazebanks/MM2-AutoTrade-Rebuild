# YouTube evidence for Pdiddler trading decisions

Researched 2026-11-03 UTC. Scope: evidence relevant to the user's Supreme-valued trader, strict minimum +15 profit, up to four native trade slots, immediate highest-eligible-item opening, approximately 5-second negotiation steps, 12-second empty-offer timeout, and proposed 60-second overall backstop. No runtime or design files were changed.

## Main result

The accessible guides consistently support checking current values and considering demand/trend alongside the arithmetic. They describe upgrade/downgrade cycles and broad exposure to traders. They do **not** establish that an immediate highest-value opening, exactly +15 profit, a fixed knives-over-guns preference, five-second negotiation, twelve-second empty timeout, or sixty-second cap is optimal. Those remain user requirements or testable engineering choices.

YouTube is useful for identifying the variables the planner should measure, not for proving a fixed trading policy. These are creator opinions, demonstrations, and selected outcomes. They provide no representative sample of accepted, rejected, cancelled, and profitable offers.

## Retrieval and evidence limits

All substantive claims below come from actual retrieved video transcripts, not inferred viewing. Firecrawl returned page metadata, full descriptions, and transcript text. Alexandria's declared `youtube-com / videos/video_transcript` contract supplied timed English automatic captions for the linked timestamp claims. I did not watch the videos or independently verify onscreen inventories, trade screenshots, completed trades, price forecasts, or creators' inventory-growth claims.

Six search queries were inspected; selected source provenance is preserved in [source_manifest.json](source_manifest.json). Narrow quoted searches returned no results; broader searches found relevant guides. Search feedback was submitted promptly. No failed transcript access occurred. On three multi-language videos the provider initially selected a non-English automatic track; English was present in `available_tracks` and an explicit `language: "en"` returned English. The timestamp links below were checked against English records; full caption files are not mirrored here. Automatic captions can misrecognize names or numbers.

Metadata dates below are the upload dates reported by the retrieved pages, all on or before the research cutoff. Values quoted by creators are historical examples, **not** a current item price source.

## What this supports for the proposed script

| Decision | Evidence-backed conclusion | Compatible action or open test |
|---|---|---|
| Correct values | Multiple transcript sources urge checking values and the update date before trading. Historical creator quotes are not current prices. | Keep Supreme as the chosen source; use exact identities/variants, current source publication information and one consistent per-trade valuation snapshot. Unknown/uncertain values must remain ineligible. |
| Raw profit versus quality | Several independent creators say demand and trends affect tradability beyond nominal value. None provides a verified numeric conversion from demand to liquidity or a risk haircut. | Require +15 first; rank safe plans using trustworthy item demand/status and inventory concentration. Do not invent liquidity from demand or add assumed resale premiums to Supreme totals. |
| Upgrades/downgrades | Four-slot concentration can make a larger item more useful; creators describe overpay-to-upgrade followed by profitable downgrade. | Strict +15 on every leg means our strategy will reject many market-standard upgrade offers. Preserve that boundary and disclose the opportunity cost; do not silently permit losses. Avoid turning one liquid anchor into four unwanted items merely to show +15. |
| Highest item immediately | No retrieved guide compares GUI opener policies. Open-ended offer listing advice is a different setting. | Preserve the requested behavior, but log the opening item, partner inventory, rejection/response and eventual outcome so it can be evaluated. Do not claim research has proven it optimal. |
| Most attractive safe offer | Creators describe differing preferences, set completion, demand, and concentration. No validated universal weights. | Among all eligible combinations satisfying +15, consider item quality, useful sets, slot efficiency and avoiding unnecessary extras. Reevaluate on real offer changes, not a belief that the first offered item must remain forever. |
| Knives versus guns | No reliable universal preference rule was found. High-demand gun examples appear in the same guides that discuss knives. | A knife preference may remain a user-chosen tiebreaker. Do not let it override actual item quality or price eligibility. |
| Negotiated adds | General negotiation/patience advice only. No source establishes automatic periodic additions or five seconds as optimal. | Keep 5 seconds as a configurable design choice; each changed offer needs a fresh safe plan and actual-slot verification. No repeated add without a meaningful improvement/reason. |
| +15 floor | No validated numeric floor. “Small consistent profits” is qualitative. A fixed +15 has a very different percentage impact at 50 versus 5,000. | Keep the user's strict floor; report both absolute and percentage gains for evaluation. Do not infer an alternative threshold from videos. |
| Empty 12s / final 5s / cap60s | No empirical support located for these precise timings. Creator patience advice is not a timer benchmark. | Treat them as testable operational settings and measure abandonment, latency, and completion. Preserve all state/acceptance safety requirements regardless of timing. |
| Server selection | Creator recommendations conflict; some segment public versus dedicated servers by inventory size/target. | Measure eligible counterparties, request responses, completed +15 trades, time and inventory quality gained per server type. Do not hardcode a creator's 500-value cutoff or hop during an active/pending trade. |
| Bot avoidance | These videos provide no validated appearance/movement classifier or evidence that a bot server can be identified reliably from the described patterns. | Keep this research separate from proving the bot detector or hop action correct. Runtime evidence and tests are needed. |
| Safe completion | Guides recommend ordinary in-game trades and avoiding multi-step/offsite promises; they do not validate our integration. | Only verified completed native trades should count toward inventory growth/webhooks; screenshots or closed GUI alone cannot become proof of success. |

Before using performance claims to change policy, collect outcomes for **all** eligible attempts, including declines, empty offers, timeouts and unconfirmed outcomes. Separate value-list changes from value gained by completed trades. Record item-quality inputs as sourced fields, not guaranteed sale prices. This is an inference for evaluating an automated trader, not a workflow demonstrated by these videos.

The strongest useful finding is qualitative: measure inventory quality and counterparties' real responses in addition to nominal Supreme profit. No retrieved YouTube source validates a complete profitable autonomous trader or supplies its acceptance, session, completion, or webhook correctness.
